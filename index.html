Solution Basics

A solution is a uniform mixture. The solvent does the dissolving and the solute gets dissolved.

Solvent

The larger component. In saltwater, water is the solvent.

Solute

The smaller component. In saltwater, NaCl is the solute.

Aqueous

Water is the solvent. Example: NaCl(aq).

Like dissolves like

Polar and ionic substances dissolve best in polar solvents. Nonpolar substances dissolve best in nonpolar solvents.

Electrolytes

A solution conducts electricity only when it has freely moving ions.

TypeWhat happens in waterExamples
Strong electrolyteCompletely makes ionsNaCl, HCl, KOH, CaCl₂
Weak electrolyteOnly partly makes ionsHF, CH₃COOH, NH₃
NonelectrolyteStays as whole moleculesGlucose, CH₃OH, ethanol

How to classify them quickly

If you see...Classification
Metal + nonmetal, or metal + polyatomic ionIonic compound. If it dissolves, it is a strong electrolyte.
HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, HClO₄, H₂SO₄Strong acids and strong electrolytes
Other acids like HF, H₂CO₃, CH₃COOHWeak acids and weak electrolytes
LiOH, NaOH, KOH, RbOH, CsOH, Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂Strong bases and strong electrolytes
NH₃Weak base and weak electrolyte
Glucose, ethanol, CH₃OHMolecular nonelectrolytes
OH trick: KOH has a metal, so it is a strong base. CH₃OH and C₂H₅OH are alcohols, not bases. They are nonelectrolytes.

Concentration Units

UnitFormulaRemember
Molarity, Mmol solute / L solutionUse total solution volume. Convert mL to L.
Molality, mmol solute / kg solventUse only solvent mass. Convert g to kg.
Mass percent(mass solute / mass solution) × 100Mass solution = solute + solvent.
Mole fraction, χmol component / total molAll mole fractions add to 1.00.
ppm(mass solute / mass solution) × 10⁶Used for very low concentrations.
ppb(mass solute / mass solution) × 10⁹Used for extremely low concentrations.

Molarity example

0.250 mol ÷ 0.500 L = 0.500 M

Molality example

0.300 mol ÷ 0.250 kg = 1.20 m

Boiling & Freezing Calculations

Boiling-point elevation
ΔTᵦ = iKᵦm
Tᵦ solution = Tᵦ pure + ΔTᵦ

Boiling point goes up. For water, Kᵦ = 0.512 °C/m and pure water boils at 100.0 °C.

Freezing-point depression
ΔT𝒇 = iK𝒇m
T𝒇 solution = T𝒇 pure − ΔT𝒇

Freezing point goes down. For water, K𝒇 = 1.86 °C/m and pure water freezes at 0.00 °C.

Every calculation: use this order

  1. Find moles of solute: moles = grams ÷ molar mass.
  2. Convert the solvent mass from grams to kilograms.
  3. Find molality: m = mol solute ÷ kg solvent.
  4. Find i by counting the ions made when it dissolves.
  5. Calculate the temperature change: ΔT = iKm.
  6. Boiling: add ΔT. Freezing: subtract ΔT.
Most common mistakes:
  • Molality uses kg of solvent, not kg of solution.
  • Do not forget i for ionic compounds.
  • Boiling = add. Freezing = subtract.
